The Most Common Passwords: According to WikiPedia

Published: July 2026 • 6 min read

Every year, cybersecurity groups compile millions of leaked credentials from database breaches around the world. This aggregation has become a formal research topic, as detailed on the official Wikipedia List of the Most Common Passwords. The statistical data reveals an alarming truth: human beings continue to reuse the exact same predictable combinations across corporate networks and personal apps.

These weak credentials do not just expose individual accounts; they are the primary targets for automated botnets executing brute-force dictionary attacks. Below, we break down the top entries from Wikipedia's real leak databases.

The Anatomy the Top 100

While the top 20 positions are dominated by simple numbers, the remaining entries in the top 100 list consistently fall into four distinct categories:

  • Keyboard Walks: Strings like qwerty123, asdfghjk, or zxcvbnm. These are chosen because they require very little finger movement across the rows, but hackers map these paths instantly.

  • Common Names: Popular names like michael, jordan, ashley, or daniel. Automated wordlists contain billions of popular name-year variations.

  • Birthday Dates: Years like (1980, 1990), or dates (12061995).

  • Pop Culture and Sports: Football teams (liverpool, barcelona), car brands (ferrari), or animal strings (dragon, monkey).
